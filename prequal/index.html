One subcontractor, several spellings

The same firm gets typed a dozen ways over ten years — Jack Ellison, Jack Ellison Paint, Paint / Jack Ellison. Left alone, one sub's record is split across six, and none of them shows the full picture. Merging them keeps every rating and every old spelling; it only decides which name they hang under.

Nothing here is guessed at. The merges already applied were misspellings, punctuation, or the scope / subcontractor format from bid-day sheets. Anything where two firms might genuinely share a word is below, unmerged, waiting for someone who knows the vendors.

Waiting on you

One pair at a time. If they are the same firm, set the name you want them to keep and merge; if they are not, mark them different and the pair will not come back. Pairs that share only a single word are hidden by default because most of them are coincidences — Ellison Painting and Ellison Plumbing & Piping are two different companies.

Names ComputerEase cut off

ComputerEase stores a vendor name in 30 characters and drops the rest — which is why Alexander Electric Company, In and Brockway Building Services, LL read the way they do. The full name is not in the ERP to recover, so these have to be typed once. Every sub whose name is exactly 30 characters is listed; some genuinely are that long, so mark those as fine and they will drop off.

The rating scale

What each score means

5Best in class. Beat the schedule or the scope, ran themselves, made the job easier. First call next time.
4Above expectations. In and out, no problems, minor issues handled without escalation.
3Met expectations. Did what the subcontract said. Normal amount of pushing.
2Below expectations. Manpower, coordination or quality problems that cost BH time or money.
1Do not use. Failed to perform, unsafe, or the relationship broke down.
The PM and the superintendent score separately and the average of the two is the sub's score for that job. Where only one of them scored, that score stands alone.

How to read an average

An average across two jobs is a hint; an average across eight is a track record. The rail shows the job count next to every score for exactly that reason — check it before you act on a number.

A single 1 or 2 buried under good scores is worth opening. That is usually where a named person, a schedule failure or a safety problem is written down, and it will not show in the average.

Twenty-six company names carried a *** prefix in the source sheet. Nobody knows what it recorded and the data ruled out every obvious reading, so it is ignored here — it affects no score, filter or sort. The prefix itself is still in the stored rows if the meaning ever surfaces.
